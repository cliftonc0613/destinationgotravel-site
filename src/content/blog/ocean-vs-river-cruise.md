When a client tells me they want to take a cruise, the first thing I try to understand is *what kind of experience they're actually looking for* — because "cruise" covers a very wide range.

An ocean cruise on Royal Caribbean's Icon of the Seas is a completely different product than a 7-night river cruise on the Danube. Both are valid. Both are genuinely wonderful trips for the right traveler. But the person who loves one doesn't always love the other.

Here's how I think about it.

## The case for ocean cruising

Ocean cruises make sense when you want scale, variety, and the feeling of a destination in itself.

The ship is the experience — especially at the mid-to-upper tier of lines like Celebrity, Princess, or Oceania. You have a full restaurant program, pools, entertainment, a spa, and dozens of ways to fill your time when you're not in port. The ports are significant destinations — Caribbean islands, Alaska fjords, the Mediterranean, the Norwegian coast.

Ocean cruising also gives you more flexibility in terms of trip length. You can find 3-night getaways or 120-day world voyages. Most of my clients find something in the 7-to-14-night range that works well.

**Ocean cruising is a strong fit if:**
- You want the ship to feel like a resort
- You're traveling with a larger group or family
- You want a wide variety of port destinations
- You're new to cruising and want to ease in

## The case for river cruising

River cruising is a fundamentally different pace. The ship is comfortable and well-designed, but it's not where you spend your time — you spend your time in the towns along the river. You dock almost every day, often in the heart of a small city, and you walk off the gangway directly into a medieval square.

This is the thing that converts people. There's no tendering, no massive crowds shuffling off a giant ship, no rushing to get back before the gangway closes. You just walk into the village and walk back when you're done.

The trade-off is that river ships are small (typically 100-190 passengers), the cabins are compact, and there's less onboard entertainment in the evenings — usually a lecturer, a local musician, or a quiet night at the bar. Clients who were expecting an ocean ship experience have occasionally been surprised. Clients who understood the tradeoff have almost universally loved it.

**River cruising is a strong fit if:**
- You want to explore Europe's interior in depth
- You prefer a quieter, more culturally focused trip
- You like small groups and personalized service
- You've been to Europe before and want to see it differently

## What about expedition cruising?

It's worth mentioning because I get asked about it more often these days: expedition cruising (Antarctica, the Galápagos, Norway's Svalbard, coastal Alaska) is its own distinct category. These are purpose-built small ships designed for access to remote places — Zodiacs, naturalist guides, wildlife in places you can't reach any other way.

If that interests you, it's worth a conversation. It tends to appeal to clients who want adventure paired with comfort, and who care more about *where* they go than about ship amenities.

## A few specific line recommendations

**For ocean cruising (premium tier):**
- **Celebrity Cruises** — excellent food, modern ships, strong Mediterranean and Caribbean programs
- **Princess Cruises** — a solid all-around option, particularly good for Alaska
- **Oceania** — for the food-focused traveler; their culinary program is genuinely exceptional

**For river cruising:**
- **Viking** — the most recognized name for good reason; consistent quality
- **AmaWaterways** — best for active travelers; bikes on every ship
- **Avalon Waterways** — distinctive open-air stateroom design, good value at their price point

I work with all of these lines and can get you access to pricing and availability that reflects your specific dates and preferences.
